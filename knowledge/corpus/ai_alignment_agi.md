# AGI and the AI Alignment Problem

The AI alignment problem is the challenge of ensuring an AI system's goals
and behavior actually match what its designers and users intend - which
turns out to be harder than it sounds, for two main reasons:

- Specification difficulty: it is hard to fully and correctly specify what
  we actually want, especially for open-ended goals - a system optimizing
  a stated metric may find unintended shortcuts that satisfy the metric
  without satisfying the underlying intent.
- Generalization difficulty: a system trained to behave well in one
  context may behave unpredictably in a new context it was not explicitly
  trained or tested on.

Discussion of artificial general intelligence (AGI) - a hypothetical system
with broad, human-level capability across many domains - raises the stakes
of alignment further, since mistakes could be harder to correct after the
fact. This is distinct from narrower, more immediate AI safety concerns
like the bias/fairness auditing done elsewhere in this project, which deal
with AI systems available and deployed today.
