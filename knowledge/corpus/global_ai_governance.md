# Global AI Governance and Standards

As AI capabilities have grown, multiple governments and international bodies
have proposed frameworks to govern its development and use, with varying
degrees of enforceability:

- EU AI Act: a risk-tiered binding regulation (unacceptable, high, limited,
  minimal risk categories), with the strictest obligations on high-risk
  systems such as those used in healthcare, employment, and law enforcement.
- OECD AI Principles: non-binding principles adopted by dozens of countries,
  emphasizing inclusive growth, human-centered values, transparency, and
  accountability.
- UNESCO Recommendation on the Ethics of AI: a global, non-binding framework
  adopted by UNESCO member states, covering human rights, environmental
  sustainability, and gender equality in AI.
- India's NITI Aayog principles: guidance rather than binding law, as covered
  elsewhere in this corpus.

A recurring tension in global governance is harmonization versus sovereignty:
a company operating across borders may face different, sometimes conflicting
obligations depending on jurisdiction, while binding treaties are difficult
to negotiate given differing national interests in AI competitiveness.
