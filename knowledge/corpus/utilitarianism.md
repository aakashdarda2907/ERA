# Utilitarian Ethics

Utilitarianism judges an action by its consequences: an action is right if
it produces the greatest good (or least harm) for the greatest number of
people, aggregated across everyone affected.

Applied to AI systems, a utilitarian lens asks: does deploying this model
reduce net harm overall, even if it makes mistakes for some individuals?
For example, a screening model that occasionally raises a false alarm but
catches many real risks may be judged favorably on aggregate-outcome
grounds, even though it is not perfect for every single case.

Criticism: utilitarianism can justify harming a minority if it benefits a
larger majority, which is why it is usually considered alongside - not
instead of - rule-based (deontological) and character-based (virtue) ethics.
