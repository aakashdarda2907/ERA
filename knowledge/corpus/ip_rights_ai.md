# IP Rights and AI

Intellectual property law faces several unresolved questions raised by AI:

- Training data: whether using copyrighted text, images, or code to train an
  AI model without a license constitutes infringement, or falls under fair
  use / fair dealing exceptions, is actively contested in ongoing litigation
  in multiple countries.
- Ownership of AI-generated output: most jurisdictions currently require a
  human author for copyright protection, meaning fully AI-generated content
  may not be copyrightable at all in some legal systems.
- Patent inventorship: several patent offices, including the US and UK, have
  ruled that an AI system cannot be listed as an inventor on a patent
  application - only a natural person can hold that status currently.
- Style and likeness: AI systems that can closely imitate a specific living
  artist's style, or a public figure's voice or likeness, raise questions
  distinct from traditional copyright, sometimes addressed under right-of-
  publicity or personality-rights law instead.

These questions remain unsettled in most jurisdictions, with significant
differences between countries in how they are currently being resolved.
