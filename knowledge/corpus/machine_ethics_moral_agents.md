# Machine Ethics and Artificial Moral Agents

Machine ethics studies whether and how machines can behave ethically, and
what "ethical" even means for a non-human agent. A widely used framework
(from philosopher James Moor) distinguishes four levels of ethical behavior
in machines:

1. Ethical impact agents - any machine whose actions have ethical
   consequences, whether or not it was designed with ethics in mind.
2. Implicit ethical agents - machines constrained by their design to avoid
   unethical outcomes, without explicitly reasoning about ethics.
3. Explicit ethical agents - machines that can represent ethical concepts
   and reason about them when making decisions.
4. Full ethical agents - agents with genuine moral agency, consciousness,
   and responsibility, comparable to a human - which no current AI system
   achieves.

Two broad approaches exist for designing more ethical machines: top-down
(explicitly programming ethical rules or frameworks into the system) and
bottom-up (training the system on examples of ethical behavior and letting
it generalize). Most real systems, including the SHAP/Fairlearn-based audit
approach used in this project, sit closer to an implicit ethical agent -
constrained by design and audit, not reasoning about ethics itself.
