# AI and Employment

AI-driven automation raises concerns about job displacement, particularly for
routine, predictable tasks (data entry, basic customer service, some clerical
and manufacturing roles), while also creating new roles in AI development,
oversight, and maintenance.

Key considerations for responsible deployment:

- Task-level vs job-level impact: AI often automates specific tasks within a
  job rather than eliminating the job entirely, changing what the role
  requires rather than removing it outright.
- Reskilling and transition support: organizations deploying AI at scale are
  increasingly expected to invest in retraining displaced workers rather than
  treating displacement as an externality.
- Uneven impact: lower-wage, routine-task roles are typically more exposed to
  automation than roles requiring complex judgment, creativity, or
  interpersonal skill, raising equity concerns about who bears the transition
  cost.

Policy responses discussed globally include portable benefits (not tied to a
single employer), universal basic income pilots, and mandated impact
assessments before large-scale automation in critical sectors like
healthcare.
