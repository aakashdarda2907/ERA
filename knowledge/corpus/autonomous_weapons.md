# Autonomous Weapons and Military AI

Autonomous weapons systems (AWS) are weapons that can select and engage
targets with reduced or no direct human control over the individual
engagement decision. This raises distinct ethical and legal questions beyond
civilian AI applications:

- Meaningful human control: a central principle in this debate is whether a
  human retains genuine decision-making authority over lethal force, rather
  than merely being able to override a system that has already acted.
- International humanitarian law compliance: questions remain over whether
  autonomous systems can reliably apply principles like distinction (between
  combatants and civilians) and proportionality in complex, changing
  environments.
- Accountability gap: if an autonomous system makes an unlawful targeting
  decision, it is unclear whether responsibility falls on the operator, the
  commanding officer, the manufacturer, or the software developers - a
  version of the accountability problem this project addresses in a civilian
  healthcare context, but with far higher stakes.
- Arms-race dynamics: several states and civil-society groups have called for
  a preemptive international treaty banning fully autonomous lethal weapons,
  similar to existing bans on chemical and biological weapons, though no
  binding global agreement currently exists.
