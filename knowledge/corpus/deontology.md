# Deontological Ethics

Deontology judges an action by whether it follows a moral rule or duty,
regardless of its consequences. Some actions are considered wrong in
themselves - for example, using a legally protected attribute (like race)
as a basis for a decision - even if doing so happened to produce a better
aggregate outcome.

Applied to AI systems, a deontological lens asks: does this system violate
a rule it should not, such as discriminating based on a protected
characteristic, regardless of whether the overall accuracy is good?

Criticism: strict rule-following can produce worse outcomes in edge cases
the rule did not anticipate, which is why deontological analysis is often
paired with utilitarian outcome-checking.
