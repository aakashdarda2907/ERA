# GDPR vs. NITI Aayog: A Direct Comparison

Both frameworks address responsible data use and AI governance, but differ
sharply in legal force and structure.

Legal status: GDPR is a binding regulation across the European Union, with
real enforcement mechanisms and penalties for non-compliance. NITI Aayog's
Principles for Responsible AI are guidance documents for India, not
legally binding, and carry no penalty structure of their own.

Scope: GDPR specifically governs the processing of personal data, with AI
regulation following as a consequence of how AI systems handle that data.
NITI Aayog's principles are framed more broadly around AI systems themselves
- safety, equality, inclusivity, privacy, transparency, accountability, and
positive human values - not exclusively data processing.

Enforcement: GDPR is enforced by independent Data Protection Authorities in
each EU member state, with fines that can reach a percentage of a company's
global annual revenue. NITI Aayog's principles have no equivalent enforcement
body; compliance is currently voluntary, though India's Digital Personal
Data Protection Act (2023) introduces separate binding obligations for
personal data specifically.

Overarching principle: both frameworks treat accountability as central -
GDPR requires organizations to demonstrate compliance with documented
evidence, while NITI Aayog lists accountability as one of seven guiding
principles - but only GDPR currently backs that principle with binding legal
consequence.
