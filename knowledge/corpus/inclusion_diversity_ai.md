# Inclusion and Diversity in AI Development

Who builds an AI system affects what problems it solves well and for whom.
Concerns in this area include:

- Team composition: AI development teams that lack diversity across gender,
  race, disability status, and socioeconomic background are more likely to
  overlook failure modes that disproportionately affect underrepresented
  groups, since these issues are less likely to be encountered during
  informal testing.
- Data representation: training data collected primarily from one
  demographic, region, or language group produces models that perform worse
  for everyone else - a data-level cause of the kind of fairness gap this
  project's own audit measures for age, race, and gender.
- Accessibility: AI-powered interfaces are not automatically accessible to
  users with visual, auditory, motor, or cognitive disabilities unless
  accessibility is a deliberate design requirement from the start, connecting
  directly to human-centered design principles covered elsewhere in this
  corpus.
- Global representation: most large AI models and datasets are developed in
  a small number of countries and predominantly in English, which can
  disadvantage languages and cultural contexts that are underrepresented in
  training data.
