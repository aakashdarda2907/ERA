Explanation Stability Audit

Does the model's explanation hold up under a small, realistic change to a patient's data — or can the same-looking prediction be justified by a completely different story a moment later?
Fairness axis (Explanation Disparity) Does the model reason differently depending on who the patient is?
Stability axis (this page) Does the model's reasoning hold up under a tiny change to what the patient's data says?
Why this page is a novel contribution

What is this?

  • We take real cached patients and apply one small, realistic change: number of prior admissions +1.
  • We recompute both the risk score and the full SHAP explanation after the change.
  • We check how many of the original top-3 explanation factors survive that tiny nudge.

Why is this different from Explanation Disparity?

  • Disparity compares reasoning across different patients (by demographic group).
  • Stability compares reasoning for the same patient, before and after a tiny realistic change.
  • Together they form two independent axes of explanation quality — fairness and stability — not one combined idea.

Why is this a novelty?

  • Grounded in published XAI-fragility findings (Ghorbani et al. 2019; Alvarez-Melis & Jaakkola 2018) — explanations can shift sharply even when predictions barely move.
  • No source in our literature review checks this for a clinical readmission model specifically, on real cached predictions.
  • It produces a concrete, checkable percentage — not a qualitative claim about trustworthiness.

How is this useful?

  • A convincing-looking explanation is not automatically a trustworthy one — this quantifies that gap directly.
  • Flags exactly which real patients have the most fragile explanations, for targeted clinical review.
  • Gives a hospital a concrete stability percentage to disclose alongside accuracy and fairness numbers.
