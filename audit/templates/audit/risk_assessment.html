1. Purpose & scope

WardAudit's underlying model predicts the likelihood that a diabetic inpatient will be readmitted to hospital within 30 days of discharge, using the UCI "Diabetes 130-US Hospitals" dataset (1999–2008). The prediction is intended to help clinical and administrative staff prioritize follow-up attention for patients flagged as high-risk — it is a decision-support signal, not an automated clinical decision, and is not intended to replace clinical judgment.

Two models were trained and compared: Logistic Regression (ROC-AUC {% if models.logreg.roc_auc %}{{ models.logreg.roc_auc|floatformat:3 }}{% else %}not yet computed{% endif %}), an interpretable baseline, and XGBoost (ROC-AUC {% if models.xgboost.roc_auc %}{{ models.xgboost.roc_auc|floatformat:3 }}{% else %}not yet computed{% endif %}), a stronger but opaque model. XGBoost is the deployed model; its opacity is precisely why it is the one audited with SHAP and Fairlearn throughout this assessment.

2. Who this system affects

Patients

Whose readmission-risk score may influence how much follow-up attention or discharge planning they receive. A wrong or unfair prediction could mean a patient who needs extra care doesn't get flagged for it, or a patient is flagged when they didn't need to be.

Clinicians & discharge planners

Who act on the model's output. They rely on the SHAP-based explanations and confidence context to decide how much weight to give a prediction, rather than treating the score as ground truth.

Hospital administrators

Who are responsible for ensuring the system is used fairly and appropriately across patient groups, and for reviewing the fairness audit periodically as new data comes in.

This means the model's correctness (not just its flag rate) differs meaningfully between age groups: younger patients are flagged high-risk at a noticeably higher rate than older patients, which is not automatically justified by the underlying clinical risk. Race and gender were both within the {{ threshold }} threshold on both metrics.

Dataset age and representativeness

The training data is from 1999–2008 — nearly two decades old. Hospital practices, treatment protocols, coding standards, and patient demographics have changed since then, so the model's learned patterns may not transfer cleanly to a current hospital population.

Model opacity

XGBoost outperforms the interpretable Logistic Regression baseline, but its internal reasoning cannot be read directly — every explanation offered to a user is a SHAP approximation of feature contribution, not a verified causal account of why the model made a specific prediction.

Small-sample groups

Some race categories (e.g. Asian, Unknown) and youngest/oldest age brackets have far fewer patients than the majority groups. Fairness metrics computed on small subgroups are less statistically stable and should be interpreted cautiously rather than treated as precise.

4. Mitigations & safeguards already in place

  • Every case-level answer the chatbot gives is grounded in cached SHAP and Fairlearn evidence with a citation — nothing about a specific patient is freely generated.
  • Fairness is audited across three protected attributes (race, gender, age bracket) with an explicit, consistent threshold ({{ threshold }}) for flagging a gap as material.
  • The per-group breakdown (see the model & fairness report) is published alongside the summary statistic, rather than hiding subgroup detail behind a single number.
  • This risk & impact assessment itself is a standing artifact intended for periodic review, not a one-time checkbox — it should be revisited whenever the model is retrained on new data.
  • The system is positioned as decision support only; flagged predictions are intended to prompt clinician review, not to trigger automated action.

5. Recommended human oversight

Given the age-bracket fairness gap above, any deployment decision should include a human review step specifically for younger patients flagged as high-risk, to check whether the flag reflects genuine clinical risk or a pattern the model has overgeneralized. More broadly, the fairness audit ({{ '/report/' }}) should be re-run and reviewed every time the model is retrained, not only at initial deployment.
