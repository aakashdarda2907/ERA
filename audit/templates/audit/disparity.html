Explanation Disparity Analysis

Every fairness audit so far has asked whether the model's predictions are fair across groups. This page asks a different question: does the model reason about patients differently depending on who they are — even when its accuracy looks acceptable for everyone?
Why this page is a novel contribution

What is this?

  • For every feature, we compare its SHAP contribution distribution inside one demographic subgroup against the distribution across the whole population.
  • The gap between those two distributions is measured with the Wasserstein distance — a real statistical divergence, not a visual guess.
  • A high distance means: the model leans on that factor very differently for this group of patients.

Why is this different from our fairness audit?

  • Fairlearn (elsewhere in this project) checks whether outcomes are fair — similar flag rates, similar error rates across groups.
  • This page checks whether the reasoning is fair — a model can pass every outcome check while still weighing the same clinical factor unevenly by group.
  • Outcome fairness and explanation fairness are measuring two different things, and neither implies the other.

Why is this a novelty?

  • Our literature review (SHAP, Fairlearn, ART, GDPR, NITI Aayog) never poses this specific question — every source audits outcomes, not explanations.
  • It extends the fairness-auditing question from "are the outcomes fair?" to "is the reasoning fair?" — a distinct, underexplored angle in applied XAI.
  • Every claim is a concrete, falsifiable number (a real distance, a real subgroup, a real feature) — not a qualitative impression.

How is this useful?

  • Flags exactly which factor and which group need closer clinical review — not just "the model might be biased."
  • Lets a hospital catch a subtler failure mode: correct-looking predictions built on unevenly-applied reasoning.
  • Gives an auditor a ranked, prioritized list (below) of where to look first, instead of manually inspecting every feature/group pair.
